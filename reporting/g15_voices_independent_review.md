## Rights, source-chain and ethical boundary

- Each news story is copyrighted. **Short, accurate, source-linked editorial excerpts** can be considered with clear attribution and enough surrounding context; the existence of a public page is not a license for article screenshots, full quote cards, publisher logos, photos or repeated long passages. Prefer original paraphrases for AP and for additional speakers in the same Mongabay article. The TNIE, Mongabay, AP and Indian Express snippets are source-identification quotes, not republishing permissions or a general legal conclusion.
- The original interviews belong to their credited reporters. Public copy must say **“told The New Indian Express,” “Mongabay reported,” “AP interviewed”** or equivalent, never imply India Water Watch met these people, witnessed the scenes or verified their private losses. Dates should be visible on every account. Name the known place, avoid inventing a missing village/district, and avoid pinpointing vulnerable people's shelters.
- Reuters syndication, AP partner republications and newspapers repeating a politician's letter are **one source chain each**. They do not independently corroborate the reported interview, a production forecast or actual payment. The checked TNIE and Mongabay stories are distinct original reporting chains. A sampled news collage is not a prevalence estimate.
- The reviewed TNIE, Mongabay, AP, Indian Express and local reports demonstrate **active media coverage**. A claim that news channels are silent about the subject is contradicted by the inspected source set. The editorial question is where coverage is specific, current and independently measured—not whether no one reported it.
