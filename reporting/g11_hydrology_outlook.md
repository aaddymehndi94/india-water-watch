## India Water Watch conditional outlook through the next monsoon

**Editorial status:** Proposed original qualitative **scenario**, explicitly **not an official forecast, probability, or asserted outcome**. Horizon: **late September 2026 through the onset and course of the 2027 southwest monsoon**; exact onset and rainfall are unknown. Starting evidence is the IMD 23 Sep rainfall graphics, the **older** CWC 10 Sep monitored-storage bulletin, and the 24 Sep event report. The 24 Sep heavy rainfall may change local catchment and storage conditions; no current system balance is verified.

> **Conditional prediction:** Where a supply system enters the post-monsoon months with low *usable* storage or depleted matched wells, and subsequent catchment inflows fail to replace releases, withdrawals and evaporation, its buffer against interruptions is likely to shrink before the next substantial local replenishment season. Where late September and October–December rain reaches the relevant catchments, reservoirs recover and delivered service remains verified, that concern should ease. This site cannot yet say which districts or households will follow either path, or whether 2027 will be dry.

- **Mechanism and confidence:** The direction follows the physical balance `next usable storage = current usable storage + measured inflow − releases − evaporation − other losses`, with operating and capacity constraints. **High confidence only in that conditional direction**, assuming all terms have the stated sign and a fixed reservoir/service system. **Low/unknown confidence in any geographic or calendar outcome**, because current usable capacity, reservoir operations, transfers, extraction, utility service and future rainfall are not verified. The CWC live-storage statistic is not necessarily all usable supply.
- **Sustained-stress branch:** If the 10 Sep CWC southern/basin deficits persist in a **specified verified cohort** after the recent storm and the later replenishment season is weak in its catchments, operators may face tighter allocation choices as stock is drawn down. This is a question to test in each service area, not a national warning or assertion that taps will fail. A system could also be buffered by transfers, groundwater, demand management or additional late rain; none is assumed effective without records.
- **Recovery branch:** If CWC same-cohort storage and monitored wells improve after rainfall **and** the relevant utility documents stable quantity, quality and service hours, the concern would be downgraded for that system. The 24 Sep deep-depression report means rapid local rainfall is already occurring, but its hydrological benefit must be observed, not presumed.
- **Compounded branch:** A system with low confirmed usable storage, continued high withdrawals, poor water quality or infrastructure damage could face constraints despite some rainfall. This branch requires independent service and quality records and makes **no numerical shortage, crop-loss, mortality, economic-loss or political prediction**.
- **Disproof or revision triggers:** (1) CWC 17/24 Sep and subsequent same-cohort reports show material verified replenishment; (2) the forthcoming October–December IMD outlook and then observations favor replenishment in the relevant catchment; (3) well levels and extraction/resource assessments with matched networks show recovery; (4) utilities show reliable delivered volume, hours and quality; (5) conversely, verified declines in these indicators escalate concern. A national rainfall departure alone neither proves nor disproves a local supply outcome.
- **Missing inputs before quantitative prediction:** reservoir IDs/capacity revisions, inflows/outflows and dead/allocated storage; basin and service-area crosswalks; withdrawals/evaporation; groundwater well IDs/aquifer properties and observation dates; utility delivery/quality logs; future IMD seasonal products and their skill/uncertainty. No “days left,” shortage probability, district ranking or 2027 rainfall value can be calculated responsibly from the present inputs.
