## The editorial diagnosis

The current first screen has striking type and a source-linked number, but its opening question, **“The rain is down. What happens next?”**, is broad. The approved June rank—arguably the first finding a reader will repeat to someone else—is absent from the homepage. The following chapters mostly alternate text and similarly shaped number cards. The reader never *sees* a June historical comparison, the four regional gaps change side by side, an administrative order become a delivery outcome, or a causal path from rain to a tap. The presentation asks for attention before giving a visual discovery. On mobile, the opening consumes almost a screen before the first figure is explained. The hero art is clearly labeled as symbolic, but it cannot carry evidence or substitute for an actual data visual.

The live narrative is also too tidy for the evidence. It progresses rainfall → reservoirs → Karnataka → taps → future, yet the record contains a more compelling tension: a historically low **June**; a season-to-date national shortfall that varies sharply by region; contemporaneous destructive heavy rain and flood response elsewhere; 177 administrative drought declarations in Karnataka; and no verified count of people whose taps failed. That is the story's shape. The contradiction is real and understandable if every clock, denominator, and place is visible.

## Opening to use

**Headline:** “One monsoon. Two kinds of emergency.”
**First screen:** “June 2026 was India's sixth-lowest-rainfall June since 1901, according to IMD. By 23 September, the national rain tally remained 14.9% below normal for those dates. Karnataka had declared 177 taluks drought-affected; elsewhere, official reports described flood evacuations. What happened between the rain, the reservoirs, and people's water?”

This is a set of **separate dated findings**, not an assertion of nationwide household shortage. The headline would need a nearby deck specifying the different locations and emergency types. A narrower alternative if “emergency” overstates the administrative record: **“India's uneven monsoon, in seven records.”** The opening should use one large June rank and a scroll transition to the September cumulative position; do not animate the number as if a live sensor were counting. Sources immediately adjacent: `C-IMD-JUNE-RANK-INDIA-2026` / `E-IMD-JUNE-SUMMARY-2026`; `C-IMD-RAINFALL-DEP-india-20260923` / `E-IMD-ALLINDIA-20260923`; `C-KA-DROUGHT-THREE-ORDERS-20260922` / three Karnataka gazettes; `C-MOD-FLOOD-RESCUE-20260726` / `E-MOD-FLOOD-RESCUE-20260726`. Label the flood item **agency-reported, July, Gujarat/Dadra & Nagar Haveli**, so it does not appear simultaneous with September rainfall.

## Presentational rules that matter for this story

1. **One label vocabulary:** `IMD observed/reported`, `CWC reported`, `Government order`, `Agency-reported action`, `Historical comparison`, `Official forecast`, `AI Author assessment`, and `Unknown`. Pills must carry date/scope; color cannot be the sole distinction. “Measured” should not erase that some values are provider-reported graphics.
2. **A number moves only to reveal its units and basis.** Count to exactly 102.5 mm, −14.9%, 177, or ₹57 only after the static value is already present for reduced motion and assistive technology. Do not use rolling counters that resemble a continuous live feed.
3. **No empty spectacle.** Each transition should answer a reader question: which period, which region, which denominator, which stage, or what evidence would change a forecast. Freeze the preceding comparison while new evidence enters so the reader can compare rather than chase moving cards.
4. **The clipping treatment must be original summaries.** Article screenshots, photos, news channel logos, quotations from farmers or politicians, and field scenes require actual sourced records plus rights/context review. Generate no fake paper front page or fabricated quote. If a licensed photograph is later added, identify its creator, outlet, event date and location in the caption; an AI illustration must stay explicitly symbolic.
5. **One honest unanswered question is a stronger ending than an invented worst case.** The audience should leave knowing exactly what happened in June, what the late-September indicators say, what the orders authorize, what is unmeasured, and the specific observations that would change the next update.

## Unsupported premises requiring separate reporting

- **“The whole country faces a crazy water shortage” / “millions of taps will fail until next monsoon.”** The approved record covers rainfall regions, a monitored reservoir cohort, administrative orders, and older access surveys. No matched current household-service or groundwater series establishes that claim.
- **“News channels are not reporting it.”** No systematic media coverage audit exists. The site cannot assert neglect, suppression, or motives from this ledger. A press-collage desk can document which outlets reported which events and what their primary sources were after article retrieval/rights review.
- **Statements from politicians, farmers, and photographs published by other news channels.** None are approved quotation/photo evidence here. Obtain exact article or public statement, speaker, date, outlet, local-language context, and reuse rights; direct project interviews require actual contact and attribution.
- **“The stock market is already affected” or “onion prices rose because of drought.”** No matched causal market analysis or 2026 water-linked loss is approved. The DCA price is real but one centre and two dates; the separately reviewed planting-area record does not establish a link to that price, rainfall, or listed firms.
- **A 2026 full-season historical rank.** The current rainfall image ends 23 September. The reviewed historic South Peninsula rows are completed through 30 September. The completed **June** rank is valid and much stronger than a fake all-season “worst since” claim.
