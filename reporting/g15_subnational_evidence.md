# G15 subnational evidence audit — 24 September 2026

Agent research only; no claim, data record, page or release was promoted. The checked sources measure different things. Groundwater resource assessment, a 2024 household tap survey, and cumulative rainfall must remain separate from each other and from September 2026 delivered supply.

## Recommendation

**Narrow candidate:** add a source-linked, explicitly historical **Karnataka 2024 rural tap-service fact** to a state page or service explainer: the Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) state factsheet estimated **96.4% working taps** (water in the preceding seven days), **91.1% supply on the scheme schedule**, and **72.6% in its adequate-quantity category** among its sampled frame of already declared Har Ghar Jal villages. It covered **744 villages and 8,928 households across 31 source-named districts** in July–October 2024. These are separately estimated weighted indicators, not the percentage of Karnataka's current rural homes receiving all three services. They cannot establish a 2026 shortage, a monsoon effect, or a trend against administrative connection counts. The national JJM report is already in the approved ledger; this would add a genuinely subnational but dated lens.

**Possible district callout after independent editorial/rights review:** the same report lists **Koppal 48.9%** in its estimated adequate-quantity category (PDF p. 26, zero-index 25) and lists **276 households with piped-scheme taps** in its district sample (PDF p. 24, zero-index 23). The quantity measure used flow at **one household per hamlet/habitation** and reported supply duration, so 276 must **not** be called the number of independent flow measurements. Do not rank districts or call the 48.9% a present-day service level. The report supplies district names, not stable administrative IDs or a boundary crosswalk. Keep district values in a labeled source-native table, not a choropleth joined by name. The report's factsheet says `≥55 LPCD`, while the later quantity table heading says `>55 LPCD`; quote the report's category as “adequate quantity” until the boundary convention is reconciled.

**IMD district layer:** current cumulative page is *not* safe to label uniformly “through 24 September.” Parse and expose each row's date and missingness first. A tightly filtered table of **722 numeric rows explicitly dated 24 September** may be technically possible, but the map layer should remain held until IMD map geometry rights, district boundary IDs, and the two arithmetic anomalies below are reviewed. Do not infer household water from these precipitation departures.

## CGWB groundwater: useful context, still a held subnational layer

The [CGWB 2025 resource-assessment index](https://cgwb.gov.in/en/ground-water-resource-assessment-0) explicitly offers state fact sheets and a block-wise categorization for GWRA-2025. It defines extraction stage as annual groundwater extraction divided by annual extractable groundwater resource. These are **annual modeled resource/extraction assessments at assessment-unit scale**, not well levels, aquifer volume remaining, delivered household service, or September 2026 conditions. A state/block category does not transfer to every district or household.

The earlier independent local audit in `reporting/groundwater_public_claims.md` verifies the national 2025 PDF and flags inconsistent metadata on the separate January 2026 well-level PDF. In this pass, a normal certificate-verifying GET of the CGWB official download failed with `SSLCertVerificationError`; no verified alternate download, block file ID/hash, block-version crosswalk, or bulk reuse terms were established. Do not disable TLS in an update adapter or label a 2025 assessment “live groundwater.” A human or browser-validated manual import of the exact official PDF with a saved hash and unit-level review is the next legitimate route. This is a **precise blocker for a CGWB map**, not a blocker for the JJM brief fact or IMD row-date QA.
