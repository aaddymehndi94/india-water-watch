**Desk date:** 24 September 2026. **Status:** candidate reporting for independent fact and copy review; no contact, interview or human review. This memo asks what authorities have actually done in response to water and agriculture risk. It does not infer a national water crisis from the existence of contingency plans. ## Finding that changes the current story: Karnataka's formal order

The [Karnataka e-Gazette's original five-page PDF](https://erajyapatra.karnataka.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/11325.pdf), Gazette no. 934, **Government Order RD 586 TNR 2026 (Part-1), 22 September 2026**, declares **53 additional taluks in 17 districts** drought-affected for the **2026 kharif season**. The order classifies **51 as severe and two as moderate**. It says the declaration takes effect immediately and lasts no more than six months unless revoked sooner (printed p. 3). The 53 taluks and severity labels are in the annexure, printed pp. 3–5. The two moderate entries are **Bangalore South, Bengaluru Urban** (annexure item 17) and **Bramhavara, Udupi** (item 53). The document records a 1 June–16 September assessment, 574 villages covered by its ground-truthing exercise, and a three-stage assessment; this is the state's administrative determination, not an independently measured household water-supply outcome (printed pp. 1–2).

The same order's preamble says earlier notifications declared **101 taluks on 27 August** (89 severe, 12 moderate) and **23 additional taluks on 17 September** (21 severe, two moderate), then 53 more on 22 September. **101 + 23 + 53 = 177** is an arithmetic count from the latest official order, not an independently reconciled taluk register. The earlier two original PDFs have not yet been independently downloaded; avoid publishing their taluk lists or an independently verified statewide severity split until they are checked. The 22 September order alone is fully retrieved from the government host.

**Action stage:** *notified/declared*. The order authorizes deputy commissioners, as heads of district disaster authorities, to take necessary drought relief and response measures under prevailing SDRF norms; it directs agriculture, horticulture and district officials to jointly survey crop loss; and it calls for continuing weekly assessments (printed p. 3). Authorization and direction **do not establish** any cash release, completed relief, number of households served, measured crop loss or actual drinking-water failure. Seek later fund sanction/release, district implementation and outcome documents for those stages.

**Narrow proposed public claim K-DROUGHT-20260922:** “Karnataka's 22 September gazette order declared 53 additional taluks drought-affected for the 2026 kharif season, classifying 51 as severe and two as moderate. It authorized district officials to undertake relief under SDRF norms and directed a crop-loss survey; completed assistance and losses require separate evidence.” Source: government-hosted PDF above, pp. 2–5; observation/assessment period 1 June–16 September 2026; published 22 September 2026; retrieved 24 September 2026; snapshot SHA-256 `f1453449104e8b96098c931fdf724265740b89e8c430284e69278c035fdbb6d5`. The exact page and annexure should be linked beside any card or map; taluk boundaries/denominator are not yet versioned for a choropleth.

**Counterevidence/guardrail:** An official drought classification in specified taluks cannot be extended to every Karnataka district or the rest of India. The 22 September order does not say every classified taluk currently has a drinking-water outage. The web-documented [23 September IMD Karnataka warnings](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/subdivisionwise-warning_mc.php?id=13) included heavy/very heavy rain warnings for parts of Karnataka; these are forecast warnings, not proof that drought effects had ended. Verify the warning's archive/issue time before using it as an enduring public claim. A statewide “worst in 150 years” claim reported from a legislative speech remains **unverified** and should not be repeated as fact.
