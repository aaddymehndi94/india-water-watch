# History, population and geography evidence memo

Desk review: 24 September 2026. Scope: historical comparator and geographic/population contract. This is research, not an independent second calculation review or human editorial approval. All URLs below are primary-source locators inspected on this date. Do not turn the candidate comparisons into a 2026 rank before a matched 2026 cutoff series exists.

## Recommended publishable historical comparison

IMD Pune's [June–September regional rainfall table](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/cmpg/Product/Rainfall_Data.html) publishes monthly actual rainfall in millimetres and separate percentage departures for all India and four homogeneous regions, with rows from 1901 through 2025 (last row inspected). Its [twelve-month homogeneous-region table](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/cmpg/Product/homo.html) publishes actual monthly millimetres and a 1971–2020 normal row. The former is the most feasible **completed-season comparator** for a launch chart. Preserve the provider's region definition and table revision; sum June–September actual millimetres only where all four months are present; rank like-for-like completed seasons only. Do not mix IMD homogeneous South Peninsula with a selected set of southern states or a meteorological subdivision.

Source inspection confirms a key historical nuance. In the homogeneous-region table's **South Peninsula (10 sub-divisions)** block, 2002 monthly June–September actuals are 136.4, 89.3, 172.3 and 68.9 mm, respectively ([row near line 626](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/cmpg/Product/homo.html)); their displayed one-decimal sum is 466.9 mm. The adjacent October value is 164.9 mm. Thus a southwest-monsoon figure cannot stand for that region's entire water year or northeast-monsoon contribution. This value is a direct four-month arithmetic extraction from an IMD row, **not yet independently reviewed**, and should not appear as a headline until a second calculation checks the source snapshot and normal. The separate [IMD 2024 rainfall-statistics report](https://hydro.imd.gov.in/hydrometweb/PRODUCTS/Publications/Rainfall%20Statistics%20of%20India%20-%202024/Rainfall%20Statistics%20of%20India%202024.pdf) lists district seasonal statistics and annual/monsoon series for 2000–2024, but changing districts and normals make this a separate product, not an automatic extension of the Pune series.

For a 2026 season through 23 September, IMD's complete June–September historical totals cannot establish `rank of N`: each earlier year includes 24–30 September. Obtain daily values from the **same** IMD series/footprint and calculate every year's June 1–September 23 total, or wait until the 2026 season ends. State the actual IMD observation cutoff and provisional revision status. A monthly only chart may compare **June–August 2026** against completed June–August historical totals and show September separately as provisional. The full 1901–2025 series has 125 nominal years; actual included N requires a missingness and revision audit before publication. A count of years as dry is an empirical sample fraction, not a recurrence probability.

An older [IMD 1901–2000 district/state/subdivision monthly compendium](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/library/public/e-book110.pdf) exists. It is **not** a separately corroborating observation network and should not be spliced with later live district feeds without geography/station-method checks. Nineteenth-century rainfall reconstructions and documentary famine accounts should be a separate timeline layer with their own source quality and uncertainty; no nineteenth-century rank against the modern regional series. The historical case selection should include counterexamples where post-monsoon rain, storage, irrigation, public response or service systems altered consequences; rainfall alone does not explain death, hunger or household supply.

## Population vintages and limits

The latest **completed census table verified here** is ORGI's [2011 Primary Census Abstract state/district table](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/6191), with administrative codes and 2011 counts. ORGI's [A-02 decadal-variation table](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/43333) explicitly says earlier counts from 1901–2001 are adjusted to **2011 jurisdictions**, making that a defensible historical geographic vintage for population trends (subject to checking each output row). The [2011 population finder](https://censusindia.gov.in/census.website/en/data/population-finder) offers state, district and finer PCA downloads. A [June 2025 Gazette notification](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/45572/download/49769/GAZ_NOTIFICATION_07.pdf) declares the next population census for 2027, generally referenced to 1 March 2027, with specified snow-bound areas referenced to 1 October 2026; no 2027 enumerated count should be implied available as of this memo.

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare's [Technical Group population projection report, 2011–2036](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Population%20Projection%20Report%202011-2036%20-%20upload_compressed_0.pdf) provides India/state/UT projections and multiple reference-date tables. Choose one explicitly (for example 1 July 2026); retain units of **thousands**, sex columns, table number and the report's vintage geography. The Ministry's [Health and Family Welfare Statistics in India 2023, table B.1.7](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Publication%20Health%20and%20Family%20Welfare%20Statistics%20in%20India%202023.pdf) reproduces 1 July projections and labels 2026 India total 1,425.90 million; this is a **projection**, not a census observation or district count. It should not be mixed with another projection table's 1 March/1 October figures. The inherited southern-state 2001/2026 totals need a full row-and-column audit and geographic crosswalk before use. The projection report may inherit pre-reorganization state groupings; check its footnotes and do not silently assign pre-split Andhra Pradesh to present-day Telangana or pre-split Jammu & Kashmir to Ladakh.

For state-level analysis, compare projected state populations to 2011 census counts only after documenting the exact administrative combination and projection method; mark the line break in visual style. At district level, the 2011 census counts are **2011 geography** and cannot simply be joined to 2026 district codes. Do not proportionally spread state projections to districts and call them measured. An exposure count needs a matched spatial layer and should be phrased as people **living in an area with an observed rainfall indicator**, never people experiencing shortage. The old `population ratio × rainfall-fraction ratio` is neither demand nor water stress. If used at all, it is an illustrative, non-hydrological sensitivity calculation with explicit assumptions.

Demand/resilience comparison should ask for observed changes in irrigation, crop mix, industry, urban distribution, reservoir capacity, groundwater extraction, reuse and service reliability, with common periods and units. A [Department of Water Resources 2026 document](https://www.jalshakti-dowr.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/71facba772426d78db2d39f8c0c2a34b.pdf) references CWC's *Assessment of Water Resources of India 2024* and separately reports a 2025 high-demand scenario; these are **modeled national quantities with differing definitions**, not proof of a local 2026 shortage. Retrieve the original CWC assessment and scenario methods before any chart. Infrastructure expansion and post-monsoon recovery are plausible counterevidence and must be checked, not assumed away.

## Geographic identifiers, boundaries and rights

Use a versioned contemporary state/UT and district inventory from the government [Local Government Directory download](https://lgdirectory.gov.in/demo/downloadDirectory.do). LGD provides entity downloads and modifications; capture export date, LGD code, parent code, status and name aliases. The page presents a CAPTCHA for some reports, so a reproducible adapter may need a permitted manual-import snapshot. Preserve census 2011 codes separately; build a reviewed crosswalk with split/merged district relationships. Never string-join district names alone. Meteorological subdivisions, basins, districts and utility service areas are distinct many-to-many geographies. A region such as IMD South Peninsula must remain its native footprint unless an explicit weighted intersection exists.

Survey of India lists a national **state-to-district shapefile** (product OVSF/1M/7, 1:1 million, ₹0) in its [online catalogue](https://onlinemaps.surveyofindia.gov.in/Digital_Product_Show.aspx). Its [FAQ](https://onlinemaps.surveyofindia.gov.in/FAQs.aspx) says administrative boundaries can be downloaded without login. An [alternate SoI page](https://surveyofindia.gov.in/pages/administrative-boundary-data-base-abdb-) links a pan-India state/district/subdistrict RAR; the browser retrieval timed out on 24 September 2026. **Free download is not a verified reuse license.** SoI's current [copyright policy](https://surveyofindia.gov.in/pages/copyright-policy) says reproduction in whole or part requires written permission and specifies attribution. Its [geospatial guidelines page](https://onlinemaps.surveyofindia.gov.in/GeospatialGuidelines.aspx) says SoI political maps/digital boundaries are the standard and digital display/printing are permissible. These texts need rights/legal reconciliation before bundling a public TopoJSON or ZIP; no permission has been obtained by this review. A non-map state/UT table and search are the safe publication route until rights and cartographic requirements are resolved. The date and actual license terms of any boundary archive also need inspection.
