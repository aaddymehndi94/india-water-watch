# Public-claim candidates: drinking-water access and functionality

Prepared 24 September 2026. Desk source check by an agent; **not human review** and not a 2026 shortage assessment. These are narrow sentence candidates for an editor to integrate with claim IDs and visible source links. All three official sources were reopened on 24 September 2026. The JJM national PDF was separately downloaded to a temporary local file and its text checked against the cited pages; it is not included in the public build.

## Proposed public prose

### AC-01 · Historical household access (reported, 2018)

> In a national household survey conducted from July to December 2018, 58.2% of rural households and 80.7% of urban households reported a drinking-water facility within their premises.

**Source:** [National Statistical Office, NSS Report 584 press note](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Press%2BNote_Report%2BNo%2B584_DWS_76th%20Final.pdf), issued **23 November 2019**, p. 1 §3 (central sample: 106,838 households; 63,736 rural and 43,102 urban) and p. 2 §3.1(d) (percentages). **Scope:** estimated national rural and urban household shares for the 2018 survey period, based on household responses. **Counterevidence and limit:** the same survey says 87.6% of rural and 90.9% of urban households reported sufficient water through the year from their principal source (p. 2 §3.1(c)); this does not identify 2026 conditions, measured water quality, daily service hours or individual coping costs. Use the sufficiency result nearby if describing historic gaps, to avoid implying most households reported shortage.

### AC-02 · Historical sufficiency (reported, 2018)

> In that 2018 survey, 87.6% of rural households and 90.9% of urban households said their principal source supplied sufficient drinking water throughout the year.

**Source:** [NSO press note](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Press%2BNote_Report%2BNo%2B584_DWS_76th%20Final.pdf), p. 2 §3.1(c), with dates and sample on p. 1 §3. **Scope:** self-reported sufficiency from a principal source, not continuous utility service or an independently measured safe-water outcome. **Counterevidence and limit:** fewer households had a facility on premises (AC-01); availability at one source does not describe collection burden or service reliability. Do not subtract these percentages to estimate the number short of water in 2026.

### AC-03 · Reported rural tap connections (reported administrative count, March 2026)

> States and Union Territories reported that about 15.82 crore of India's roughly 19.36 crore rural households had tap-water supply at home as of 3 March 2026, or 81.71%.

**Source:** [Ministry of Jal Shakti written Rajya Sabha reply, published 30 March 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246883&lang=1&reg=6), paragraph 2, release ID 2246883 / RS USQ 4062. **Scope:** rural household administrative count at the stated date, **reported by states/UTs**; the reply compares it with 3.23 crore reported at JJM's start in 2019. **Counterevidence and limit:** the count does not measure hours, volume, water quality or whether every listed tap worked. The 2024 sampled functionality assessment (AC-04) found gaps among declared-covered villages. The 2018 NSO figure (AC-01) used a different survey question and population frame, so do not plot these as a matched growth series or assert that coverage expansion solved access problems.

### AC-04 · Sampled functionality components (observed/reported survey, July–October 2024)

> A 2024 survey of villages already recorded as covered by Jal Jeevan Mission found that, among surveyed households with tap connections, 86.5% had received water from the tap in the preceding seven days and 83.6% reported supply on their scheme's schedule. The report estimated that 80.2% received at least 55 litres per person per day; 76% of sampled household water passed its microbiological test.

**Source:** [JJM, *Functionality Assessment of Household Tap Connection—National Report 2024*](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/FHTC_National%20Report%202024.pdf), national factsheet p. 2; field dates §2.3 p. 8; population/sample §3.1 p. 10; weighting §3.8 p. 20; exclusions §4 p. 21; working-tap definition §4.3 p. 28; regularity §4.4 p. 30; quantity §4.5 pp. 31–32; water-quality methods and results §4.6.1 p. 33. [JJM report index](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/functionality-report-2024), national report row. **Scope:** independent third-party survey conducted July–October 2024 in a frame of villages already listed as Har Ghar Jal as of **1 May 2024**, excluding villages with fewer than 20 households. The survey initially covered 19,812 villages and 237,608 households in 761 districts/34 states and UTs, excluding Delhi and Chandigarh. It removed 411 sampled villages without household taps from the analysis, leaving 19,401 villages and 232,691 households; household percentages are weighted. Quantity was calculated from flow measured at one household per hamlet/habitation and supply duration, so 80.2% is a survey estimate rather than a measurement in every home. Water samples were taken from two households per source where a working tap was reported, tested for E. coli and total coliform in accredited labs, with pH measured on site. Chemicals were tested at sources separately. **Counterevidence and limit:** these are **not** all-rural-India prevalence estimates. “On schedule” does not mean continuous supply. The report's 76% “functional” figure is calculated as the *minimum* of separately estimated quantity, quality and regularity percentages because those criteria were not jointly measured for every household (§4.7 p. 36). Do not state that 76% of households were observed to meet all three, or that 76% of homes had fully safe drinking water. A ministry [reply from 29 January 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2220145) summarizes the survey but gives less methodological detail than the report.

## Publication controls and rights

- Attach source links **at each sentence** and display the observation period separately from the 2019/2026 publication dates, the 24 September 2026 retrieval/review date and any later deployment date. These are static, dated findings, not a live sensor feed.
- Use short original paraphrases and an original chart if needed. Link to the official PDFs; do not redistribute their full text, tables, branding, photographs or page images without a rights check. Government hosting is not proof of unrestricted reuse. The percentages alone may be cited with attribution and accurate scope.
- Do not infer a 2026 shortage, potability finding, district pattern, class effect, illness or economic loss from these three sources. A current local claim needs dated local service and water-quality evidence.
- If the four sentences appear together, explain that the NSO survey, JJM administrative count and JJM declared-village sample have **different denominators and methods**. Do not combine them into a single time-series chart.
