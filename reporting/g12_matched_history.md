# G12: Can the 2026 rainfall snapshot be ranked against the past?

**Research status:** Candidate research for independent source and arithmetic review, 24 September 2026. No rank for 1 June–23 September 2026 is approved by this memo.

## Question and result

The currently approved India and IMD South Peninsula rainfall observations end on **23 September 2026**. The official long homogeneous-region table lists **complete calendar months** and hence complete 1 June–30 September seasons for 1901–2025. Its 2002, 2009 and 2025 values cannot supply the same 23 September cutoff. A claim such as “worst since 2002” or a `rank of 126` for the ongoing 2026 season would compare seven extra September days in earlier years and must be held.

IMD does provide a **narrower completed-month result**: its June 2026 monthly climate summary reports **102.5 mm all-India rainfall versus 165.3 mm normal**, and identifies June 2026 as the **sixth-lowest June since 1901**. The latest earlier June lower than 102.5 mm in the separate current IMD historical monthly HTML is **2014 (92.1 mm)**. This supports a carefully scoped “June was last lower in 2014” treatment after independent review; it does **not** rank the current monsoon, describe regional South Peninsula conditions, or measure water supply/impact.

## June-only arithmetic audit

The primary **June 2026 PDF p. 1** directly lists the five lower Junes: 2009 **87.6**, 1905 **91.9**, 2014 **92.8**, 1926 **96.7**, and 1923 **101.6** mm. Its most recent lower year is therefore **2014**, without an inferred cross-product rank. Separately, the preserved `homo.html` all-India table gives the same five **pre-2026** years below the 102.5 mm threshold, but somewhat different values: 1905 **92.3**, 1923 **101.7**, 1926 **97.4**, 2009 **87.5**, and 2014 **92.1** mm. I parsed the exact cached table with `BeautifulSoup`, interpreted year rows only, and compared displayed one-decimal `Jun` values as decimals; 125 of 125 1901–2025 rows had a numeric June cell. Thus `1 + count(prior June < 102.5) = 6`, consistent with IMD's reported rank. No prior displayed tie appears at 102.5 mm. The difference of up to 0.7 mm among these listed years signals revision/product/rounding differences; the HTML replay is a **consistency check**, not a merged rank input.

`100 × (102.5 / 165.3 − 1) = −37.9915…%`, or about **38% below that June normal**. This is a single month and its 1971–2020 normal, not the June–23 September departure (−14.9% all India), June–September season or a water stress measure. The June rank alone is not a prediction for September or the following dry season. The official June summary itself reports intense events during an overall low month, another reason not to equate a national aggregate with every locality.

The same PDF p. 1 additionally calls **East & Northeast India's 202.6 mm** its **second-lowest June since 1901**, after 2009's 198 mm, and **Central India's 84.4 mm** the **seventh-lowest June since 1901**. These are recorded as research leads, **not proposed for promotion in this gate**. They cannot be applied to the 23 September cumulative deficits. Its p. 3 table records **South Peninsula June 129.3 mm versus 161.0 mm normal (−20%)** but does not supply a June historical rank for that region; do not manufacture one from a different series without checking product alignment.

## Why a 23 September rank is not yet available

1. **Cutoff:** `homo.html` contains complete Septembers; the current GIF ends 23 September. September 24–30 can change historical order and the current final total. The current departure uses a **daily cumulative** normal; the monthly table's September normal is full-month.
2. **Measurement product:** the 1° and 0.25° gridded daily archives are reconstructions/analyses. The current operational all-India and homogeneous-region GIFs have no documented reproducible mask/weighting bridge in this repository. The 1° official archive description ends at 2024, precluding a direct 2026 continuation.
3. **Geography:** IMD South Peninsula is an official homogeneous *meteorological region of ten subdivisions*, not the CWC “southern” reservoir cohort, selected southern states, or IITM's older macro-region. The historical spatial footprint must be held constant and checked across boundary/metadata changes.
4. **Normal/series:** the current national GIF prints 1971–2020. A historical rank can sort **absolute comparable rainfall** without a normal if all years share a measurement definition. An anomaly rank additionally requires one fixed daily 1 June–23 September normal computed across all years. The 2026 regional graphic lacks printed cumulative normal totals.
5. **Coverage/revisions/rights:** no per-year station coverage or uncertainty accompanies the monthly HTML; operational and archive revisions may differ. Only brief attributed facts have had packaging review. Raw grids or bulk table publication require a separate rights decision.

## Exact next reproducible route

For a true 23 September ranking, acquire an official single-version **daily all-India and 10-subdivision South Peninsula 1 June–23 September series for 1901–2026**, with methodology and permissible use; or acquire IMD's matching historical and 2026 daily grids plus official region mask, area weights, missingness and normal, then reconcile every current output to IMD's published figures before ranking. A narrower 2015–2026 series is acceptable if it is honestly labeled `rank of N comparable years`, complete for each year, and uses the same product and cutoff. Archive the source version and hashes; exclude incomplete years; independently reproduce the sum and rank. Otherwise wait for IMD's completed 2026 June–September table, and only then compare **completed** seasons in that one table after editorial review.
