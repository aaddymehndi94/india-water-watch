# G13 markets and water: reporting memo

**Desk status:** Candidate reporting, 24 September 2026. This memo has not passed independent source review or been promoted to the public claim ledger. No investment recommendation or share-price forecast is made.

## Answer for the editor

Water variability is a **documented business exposure** for listed Indian firms; an attributable **2026 stock-market effect has not been verified** in this scoped review. The strongest current named case is JSW Steel. Its FY2025–26 integrated report calls non-availability of water a production-disruption risk and describes reservoirs, water efficiency and site-specific zero-liquid-discharge measures. A separate FY2024–25 disclosure says it had seen no water-related incidents materially affecting costs or revenues over the preceding four fiscal years. These statements can coexist: a material risk is not a realized loss. Neither supports a claim that current rainfall changed JSW's earnings or share price.

The Central Electricity Authority (CEA) provides a concrete historical sector example: its *Performance Review of Thermal Power Stations 2016–17*, Section 4, page 4.5, records **24 outages and 2,875.79 million units (MU) of potential generation loss in FY2016–17** under its “Raw Water Problem” category. The corresponding FY2014–15 and FY2015–16 entries were 11 / 3,009.9 MU and 17 / 4,151.54 MU. This is an official operational category, not proof that drought caused every outage; it is not a rupee loss or a listed-company return. The 2016–17 figure equals about 2.876 billion kWh, but display CEA's original MU unit and definition. The 2016–17 table assigns it **0.18% of maximum possible generation** for the reviewed units. In the same table, coal shortage caused 6,218.47 MU potential generation loss in FY2016–17, showing multiple concurrent mechanisms. Historical plant mix and reporting changes limit extrapolation to 2026.

### G13-MKT-01 — observed historical sector outcome

- **Proposed wording:** “CEA recorded 24 raw-water-problem outages and 2,875.79 MU of potential generation loss among thermal units in FY2016–17; it does not attribute every incident to drought.”
- **Scope:** India, thermal units covered by CEA's 2016–17 review, FY2016–17; CEA's “loss” is lost *potential generation*, not financial loss or unmet customer demand.
- **Primary source:** CEA, *Performance Review of Thermal Power Stations 2016–17*, Section 4, page 4.5 (PDF page 70): <https://cea.nic.in/old/reports/annual/thermalreview/thermal_review-2017.pdf>.
- **Source type/date:** Government statistical review for FY2016–17; original publication date is not established from the PDF. Retrieved 2026-09-24 09:13 UTC. Candidate snapshot SHA-256 `3dd354aa901c8a307b1df437a0a58e4023e3a099fc0955d9b1744881575dd41c`.
- **Counterevidence/context:** Same CEA row reports 4,151.54 MU in FY2015–16, greater than FY2016–17; same table lists several larger non-water outage causes. A raw-water problem can arise from supply operations as well as scarcity.
- **Visual:** Three bars for FY2014–15 to FY2016–17, labeled “CEA raw-water-problem potential generation loss (MU), historical”; hover exposes outage counts and source table. No projection bar.

### G13-MKT-02 — listed-company risk disclosure

- **Proposed wording:** “In its FY2025–26 annual report, JSW Steel identified non-availability of water as a production-disruption risk and said it was developing reservoirs near facilities alongside efficiency and reuse measures.”
- **Scope:** JSW Steel company risk disclosure for financial year ended 31 March 2026, not a report on September 2026 conditions.
- **Primary source:** JSW Steel *Integrated Annual Report 2025–26*, Risk Management, R7, “Utility – water and electricity,” under Impact/Mitigation: <https://www.jswsteel.in/jsw-steel-annual-report-2025-26/risk-management.html>. The R11 sustainability-risk section separately discusses potential restrictions, input costs and disruption from regional water stress.
- **Source type/date:** Company self-disclosure for FY2025–26; page publication day not established. Retrieved 2026-09-24 09:13 UTC. HTML snapshot SHA-256 `251ef6a105579ff8b4c95114950c09684269d90ff06f6425f04b3730c7b41d24`.

It describes mitigation, not a water-caused outage. Do not map the national or South Peninsular rainfall anomaly directly onto a particular JSW plant's source-water supply.

### G13-MKT-04 — disclosure method / what journalists can inspect

- **Proposed wording:** “SEBI's BRSR Core defines a water-footprint metric for listed-company disclosure; it is a route to inspect exposure, not a measure of 2026 share-price causation.”
- **Primary sources:** [SEBI July 2023 BRSR Core circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1689166456465.pdf) and [BRSR Core Annexure I](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/jul-2023/Annexure_I-Format-of-BRSR-Core_p.pdf). ## The site module I would build

**“From a dry season to a share price: five gates”**. A horizontal/vertical interactive chain shows (1) dated local water observation, (2) actual plant/catchment supply link, (3) company-notified operations impact, (4) reported financial effect, and (5) market response with attribution analysis. Users can select “Steel” or “Thermal power.” Each gate opens the source and the missing evidence. For JSW, only the **disclosure/exposure** gate is filled by current company evidence; 2026 operational, earnings and price-attribution gates read **not verified**. The historical CEA record appears in a separate, clearly dated inset, never on the 2026 axis. Show counterforces (stored water, reuse, fuel prices, demand, currency, trade policy) as toggled explanatory paths. The interaction teaches why a red rainfall map cannot become a red stock ticker. Avoid scores, target prices and simulated portfolio returns.

**Candidate headline:** “Water can stop a plant. Has this season moved a stock? We cannot yet show it.” This is direct and visually strong without claiming evidence that is absent. An alternative shorter label is “The risk is disclosed. The market effect is unproven.”
