# G11 · Economy, access and shared-dependency evidence

Research and retrieval: **24 September 2026**. This is a desk-research memo for independent source/editorial review, not field reporting, a measurement of 2026 household harm, or a forecast. Public copy below is proposed only. Cached source files are in ignored `data/candidates/g11_economy/`; do not publish them or their full text. All financial amounts retain their original nominal year.

## The strongest defensible story

Water stress can reach people through several **different** channels: a dry spell can expose a rainfed crop; unreliable piped supply can move costs and time onto households; limited groundwater access can change what farmers can grow; and a supply interruption can contribute to food-price volatility. The existence and magnitude of any one 2026 impact must be observed separately. The approved IMD June–23 September 2026 rainfall departure is **not** evidence that any of these losses has occurred. An accessible, dramatic visual should move from *weather → local source → delivered service / farm access → observed consequences*, leaving an explicitly unmeasured link where data are absent.

## Editorial synthesis by requested question

### What can money buffer?

G11-06 documents the *mechanism*: capital can buy storage, pumping, treatment and alternate deliveries during intermittent network supply. G11-05 documents local farm differences in assets, working borewells and ability to change crops or invest. These are historical observations in specified places, **not proof of current tanker prices or that wealthy people are immune**. A local comparison requires current utility hours and tariffs, tanker receipts/registered rates, source and quality records, and anonymised household coping records across tenures and incomes. No household scene or price has been invented.

### Where do shared dependencies reappear?

Even a private tank needs replenishment; a borewell depends on a local aquifer and energy; irrigation affects production and market arrivals; a household's food basket depends on regional supply chains. G11-05 provides an actual local example of borewell depletion undermining the value of an earlier private investment. G11-03–04 show food-price dynamics have **multiple** links. Present this as a sourced *dependency diagram*, with solid links for what these sources document and dotted links for 2026 links that have not been measured. The diagram must not imply equal exposure or prove a national cascading failure.

### What has actually happened to livelihoods or prices in 2026?

**Not verified in this memo.** The late-September rain snapshot alone cannot assign crop damage, wages, employment, migration, food inflation, health costs or GDP loss. The independent crop-specific path is: dated district crop calendar and sowing/irrigation area → matched weather and source-water observations → crop-condition/output series → arrivals and same-centre retail/wholesale price series → affected-household income/cost evidence. Note interventions, trade, storage, energy and input costs as competing explanations. Do not add overlapping farm, trader and retail changes into a national “loss” total. The 2025–26 agriculture advance estimates described in `reporting/economy_access.md` concern a different season and cannot validate September 2026 damage.

## Suggested visual package (candidate, no fabricated values)

1. **One source, many paths:** an interactive causal pathway from rain to water source to service/farm to prices, with every node showing its observation date and a source button. The 2026 consequence nodes currently display **“not measured”**, never zero or an ominous animated estimate.
2. **Who can buy time?** A two-column evidence strip: documented historical coping options in the 2009 guide versus measured 2018 service access and 2023–24 food-budget composition. Keep years prominent; do not put them on a fake common timeline.
3. **Historical crop-to-market lesson:** a short annotated 2022–23 sequence from the RBI annual report: weather and arrivals both moved vegetable prices, and fresh supply later eased them. Avoid extracting a numeric curve without the underlying CPI series.
4. **Gundlupet case:** a geographically pinned *2016 field-study* card, not a 2026 drought hotspot or a representative class portrait. Pair livelihood gains with depletion/debt risks and later wage-change caveat.
