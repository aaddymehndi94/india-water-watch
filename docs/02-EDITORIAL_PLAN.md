# 02 · Editorial assignment desk

Every beat below is an assignment, not a prewritten conclusion. Deliver a reporting memo before polished prose: the question, evidence, counterevidence, unresolved issues, claim IDs and planned visual. Lead editor integrates the work into a coherent publication.

## A. The national picture

Ask whether there is a rainfall deficit, where it is concentrated, whether it is recent or season-long, and what other indicators corroborate or contradict concern. Break down geographical area, administrative units and population exposure separately. A share of districts is not a share of people or land. A wet national mean can coexist with local shortages; a dry mean need not imply uniform distress.

Produce a brief with three to five supported findings, two to three uncertainties, a selected map, and a link to the source/coverage desk. Investigate recovery as well as deterioration. Never make the page red everywhere because its title contains “crisis.”

## B. Rainfall and seasonality

Cover daily/weekly changes, season-to-date totals, dry-spell duration, timing relative to sowing, spatial concentration and how intense bursts differ from sustained rain. Separate southwest monsoon, northeast monsoon, winter rain/snow and annual/water-year context where relevant. Label every seasonal window.

Explain actual millimetres versus percentage departure. Show the baseline and how an almost-zero normal can produce unstable percentage changes. Official forecast categories are not observed rainfall classes. Climate mechanisms such as ENSO/IOD, warming or circulation changes require current agency or research evidence; do not attribute a local event to one mechanism just because it is plausible.

Visuals: cumulative rainfall against a historical band at the same cutoff; monthly anomaly small multiples; dry-spell calendar where daily coverage is sound.

## C. Storage, rivers and groundwater

Inventory which reservoirs are monitored, their capacity denominator, purpose, inflow/outflow and catchment. Compare a stable cohort and the same date. A reservoir on one side of a state border may serve another place. Do not assign a city's supply from the nearest reservoir.

Keep groundwater level, annual recharge, extractable resource, extraction and assessment category separate. Explain groundwater observation lag and well-network coverage. Satellite total-water-storage anomalies cannot be labeled district groundwater measurements. River flow can offer independent context but has its own station/period/availability constraints.

Visuals: reservoir seasonal trajectories; stable-cohort storage comparison; groundwater evidence-age and coverage view; explicitly sourced city supply-chain diagram.

## D. Is the human load greater than in earlier droughts?

Start with matched population estimates, consistent service areas and the distinction between total demand and per-person use. Investigate agricultural withdrawals, irrigated area, crops, industry, household delivery, leakage, reuse and infrastructure changes. Show absolute supply/demand components only where units and periods match.

Compare 2026 with 2002 and other independently selected analogue years, without selecting only years that support the thesis. Census counts and projections must have separate styles and uncertainty notes. Historical capacity expansion is part of the story, not an inconvenient caveat. Avoid using national ratios as local measurements.

Output an honest answer in components: what has grown, what has become more resilient, what has become more exposed, and what cannot be quantified from available evidence.

## E. History and the limits of history

Build the longest defensible **comparable** series, not simply the oldest date obtainable. Audit modern instrumental series first, then regional nineteenth-century reconstructions and documentary records as distinct layers. Do not splice their normal periods or regions into one ranking.

Candidate episodes for investigation, not pre-verified analogues: nineteenth-century drought/famine periods; 1899; 1918; 1952; the mid-1960s; 1972; 1987; 2002; 2009; 2014–16; Chennai's 2019 supply crisis; 2023 southern rainfall stress; Bengaluru's 2024 shortages. Add or remove cases based on evidence and relevance. Avoid rainfall-only explanations of historical famine; investigate institutions, access, markets, public health and reporting uncertainty.

For each chosen episode: region, season, source quality, rainfall/water indicators, documented consequences, institutions' response, what is comparable now, and what is not. Historical death counts require strong source scrutiny and ranges; never project them mechanically onto modern India.

Visuals: comparable modern series with explicit cutoffs; historical timeline with separate measurement/documentary bands; transparent analogue selector rather than “worst ever” theater.

## F. Government and institutional response

Establish the current legal/administrative process from primary documents. Investigate drought declaration, water allocation, reservoir operation, drinking-water contingency plans, agriculture advisories, relief and insurance, employment support and intergovernmental coordination. Verify the responsible institution for each action at the relevant date.

Track **announced → formally notified → funded/sanctioned → money released → implemented → outcome reported**. Do not infer later stages from a press release. Include dates, scope, original order, budget denominator, implementation evidence, exceptions and unanswered questions. Record audit/court findings with their actual scope and procedural status.

“What might government do?” should distinguish documented contingency mechanisms, stated plans and historical examples. Do not predict a particular politician's actions or provide a government competence ranking. A table of sourced actions is appropriate; a best/worst-state government league table is not.

## G. Who is talking about this?

Maintain a dated, curated evidence-and-voices desk: hydrologists, meteorologists, groundwater scientists, agronomists, economists, water utilities, farmer groups, community organizations, researchers and local journalists. Verify affiliation at the time of the statement.

Separate a public statement, a peer-reviewed finding, an interview already published elsewhere, and reporting conducted by this project. Attribute quotes to their original outlet and context. Never invent direct contact. Include local-language reporting and people affected, not only English-language metropolitan commentary. Repeated wire copies are one story, not a surge in independent evidence.

## H. Unequal exposure and what money can buy

Investigate access, tariffs, tanker costs, collection time, piped reliability, housing tenure, farm size, occupational exposure and coping resources using actual surveys, public data and attributed reporting. Consider informal settlements, rural households, small farmers, migrants, women doing collection work, older people and disabled people where evidence supports relevance. Do not treat any group as uniform.

Research whether wealth provides larger storage, treatment, legal alternative supply, temporary relocation or continuity options; distinguish documented examples from general mechanisms. Show price and service variations rather than inventing a representative “rich family” or “poor family.” Water access data must not reveal vulnerable individuals' precise homes.

## I. Where wealth may stop insulating people

Investigate shared constraints: source aquifer limits, trunk water/electricity systems, water-quality failures, food markets, transport, hospitals, workforce disruption and regional supply chains. Explain the difference between buying a private buffer and replacing a common resource. Do not state that everyone faces the same risk or use moralizing collapse narratives.

Visual: an evidence-linked dependency diagram. A hypothetical household cost calculator must show editable assumptions, geography/date, what is excluded and an “illustrative, not observed” label.

## J. Economy and livelihoods

Trace supported channels through crops and livestock, rural income, food prices, power generation/cooling, industry, construction, hospitality, health costs, public finance, household spending and migration. Use crop calendars, irrigation share, sowing data, observed output and prices rather than a universal rainfall-to-GDP multiplier.

Distinguish nominal/real prices, wholesale/retail, levels/changes, calendar/fiscal years, forecasts/actuals and gross exposure/realized loss. Do not add correlated sector losses into a fake national total. Do not attribute all contemporaneous inflation to rainfall. Any causal claim needs a credible design or a clearly attributed study with limited transferability.

Visuals: observed crop/price trajectories with careful event annotations; sector exposure matrix with disclosed evidence, not numerical crisis scores; supply-chain links with sources.

## K. Water quality, health and ecosystems

Research whether shortages, contamination, sanitation disruption or heat create documented local public-health concerns. Identify appropriate official advisories and service contacts. Do not issue chemical dosing, clinical or personalized medical instructions. Rainfall maps cannot establish water potability. Floods after dry periods do not automatically end a water-quality problem.

Cover environmental flows, fisheries/wetlands and competing seasonal uses where data are available. Keep ecological evidence distinct from human withdrawal accounting.

## L. Outlook, compound events and plausible severe scenarios

Separate official near-term forecasts, seasonal outlooks, illustrative scenarios and long-term climate projections. Investigate what would have to happen for a difficult season to become a severe supply crisis: poor replenishment, persistent demand, infrastructure failures or weak subsequent rains. Also specify the recovery branch and observations that would weaken concern.

No catastrophe montage, arbitrary deaths, famine prediction, national “day zero,” unsupported chance percentages or unvalidated district forecasts. For a severe scenario, show conditions, pathways, geographic scope, horizon, evidence, uncertainty, reversal signals and interventions described in sourced plans. See the modeling contract.

## M. What can readers and communities do?

Organize by household, apartment/community, farm, business, journalist and public institution. Use current authoritative local information; separate immediate safety, routine conservation, collective preparedness and longer-term system choices. Do not place the entire burden on household behavior or imply everyone has equal capacity to conserve.

Research leak reporting, local supply advisories, safe storage guidance from competent authorities, maintenance, lawful reuse, water accounting, community support and the limits of individual action. Never advise panic buying, hoarding, illegal extraction, interfering with public supply or buying specific products to “survive.” Provide only verified contacts with a last-checked date. Unchecked numbers are not helpful.

## N. Accountability and public-interest reporting

Create a document-based question bank: who publishes usable storage data, how allocation is documented, whether promised measures have implementation evidence, what the monitoring network misses, and where supply contracts or audits can be lawfully inspected. Frame unresolved issues as questions; do not imply corruption without evidence. Right of reply is required before sensitive allegations are published. Draft requests; do not send them automatically.

## O. Recovery and what is working

Track replenishment, improvements in supply reliability, repaired infrastructure, verified demand reductions and observed outcomes of interventions. Distinguish reported success from independent evaluation and changes explained by rainfall. Include tradeoffs, costs and limits without turning the story into a policy endorsement.

## P. What we still do not know

Publish the reporting gaps: absent district demand, lagged population data, missing utilities, incompatible reservoir inventories, inaccessible historical series, uncertain boundaries and unreviewed translations. This is an index of evidence coverage, not a map of which places are safe.

## Minimum shape of each finished explainer

A precise question; plain-language answer; dated evidence; one meaningful visual where useful; geographic/temporal scope; counterevidence or alternate explanation; limitations; sources attached to claims; what to watch next; links back to exploration. Avoid repetitive generic endings and padded prose. Do not create quotations or place scenes to make a desk-researched story appear field-reported.
