# 16 · Human reporting that agents must not counterfeit

Desk research can produce a valuable sourced publication. It cannot truthfully become firsthand reporting without real human work. Maintain a separate human-reporting queue, and never let its absence force fabrication or block well-supported desk-researched modules.

## Prepare, do not send, interview briefs

**IMD/hydrology specialists:** Which current indicators are genuinely unusual? Which baseline and geographical region should a historical comparison use? How much does timing matter? Which dataset revisions or gauge gaps could change the conclusion?

**CWC/state irrigation/utility engineers:** Which reservoirs and catchments actually serve this area? What is live usable storage versus a headline percent full? What changed in the monitoring cohort? Which allocations or operating constraints matter? What public document establishes the answer?

**Groundwater researchers:** What does the assessment measure? How old is it? How do water levels differ from storage/resource estimates? Which aquifers or monitoring-network changes prevent comparison? What is unknown at district scale?

**Agronomists/farmer organizations:** Which crops and growth stages are exposed? What role does irrigation play? What evidence separates delayed sowing, damaged crops and actual yield losses? What practical advisories are current locally?

**Economists:** Which observable channels matter now? What confounders explain prices or output? What would support causal attribution? What cannot responsibly be quantified yet?

**Water-access/community organizations:** How do supply frequency, price, collection time and quality differ within the same city/district? What evidence can be shared safely? Which voices are absent from official data? Avoid collecting household details unnecessarily.

**Businesses/infrastructure operators:** Which observed interruptions/costs are attributable to water constraints? What redundancy exists? What dependencies remain shared? What claims can be documented rather than assumed?

**Disaster-management/public-health officials:** Which warning or contingency document is operative? What actions are announced, implemented or evaluated? Which verified public contact/advisory should residents use? What should this publication explicitly not advise?

## Consent and disclosure

For any real interview, record who conducted it, date, consent, recording/transcript rights, on/off-record conditions, translation and approved attribution. Keep private contact/notes outside public Git. Confirm sensitive statements and provide appropriate right of reply. Never say “we spoke to” for a quote taken from another publication.

## External expert review packet

Send, only after authorization, a compact methods note, underlying source table, questionable comparison and specific questions. Do not ask an expert to rubber-stamp “India's worst crisis.” Record whether a response was actually received and its scope. No response is not endorsement.

## Launch without original interviews

Label the work accurately as a data-led, desk-researched publication and attribute original reporters. Avoid evocative first-person scene-setting. The human reporting queue can be published as questions and data gaps where safe, not as completed reporting.
